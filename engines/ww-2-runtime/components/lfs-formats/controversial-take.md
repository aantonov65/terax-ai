FORMAT: CONTROVERSIAL TAKE
Opens with a position most people disagree with. Destroys accepted wisdom, then rebuilds from the rubble.


LFS V4.1 OUTLINE USE:
This file is the selected native shape for the outline writer. Treat the format as the container, then fill every beat with the source swipe pressure curve, product mechanism, and buyer-facing scene evidence.

The outline must preserve this format's narrator role, emotional engine, beat order, and proof weight. Each beat should name what gets said, what concrete scene or evidence carries it, where the mechanism enters, and where the product earns its first product mention.

Recovery proof, result timelines, victory scenes, and CTA confidence enter after the product or routine discovery has happened. The final script should be planned as feed-native LFS: one thought per paragraph, blank line between thoughts, `========` dividers between sections.

BEAT SEQUENCE:
1. Hook — the provocation. A direct statement that challenges what the reader believes. "[Common solution] doesn't work. It's never worked. And the companies selling it know it." The scroll-stop is the dissonance.
2. The accepted wisdom — name what everyone believes. Validate WHY they believe it. Make the reader nod before you pull the rug.
3. The destruction — dismantle the accepted wisdom with specific, mechanical evidence. Not "it's wrong" but "here's the structural reason it was always going to fail." Each piece of evidence makes the old belief harder to hold.
4. The void — the reader's model is broken. They don't have a replacement yet. Sit in the discomfort for 2-3 sentences. "So if [common solution] was never going to work... what does?"
5. The new model — the mechanism arrives as the ONLY framework that makes sense after the old one collapsed. It's not introduced as a product feature. It's introduced as the replacement model for understanding the problem.
6. The evidence — proof that the new model works. Specific numbers, sources, timelines. The controversial take requires MORE proof than other formats because the reader's guard is up.
7. The product — arrives as the implementation of the new model. "Once I understood [the new model], I started looking for..."
8. The result — framed as vindication. The narrator was right. The controversial take paid off.
9. CTA — confident tone. The narrator has earned authority through the destruction + rebuild. Less permission, more "here's what I'd do if I were you."

WEIGHT: 40% on beats 2-4 (accepted wisdom → destruction → void). The controversial take lives on the destruction of the old belief. If the destruction is shallow, the new model has no foundation. The reader must feel their current belief system crack before the mechanism can enter.

WHAT MAKES THIS DIFFERENT: The narrator is a contrarian, not a sufferer. They're slightly combative, intellectually confident. The emotional register is "I'm going to tell you something you won't want to hear" not "let me share my pain." The argument drives the format, not the emotion.
