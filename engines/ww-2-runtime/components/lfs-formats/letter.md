FORMAT: LETTER / DIRECT ADDRESS
Written TO someone specific. "Dear [person who]..." The reader IS the recipient.


LFS V4.1 OUTLINE USE:
This file is the selected native shape for the outline writer. Treat the format as the container, then fill every beat with the source swipe pressure curve, product mechanism, and buyer-facing scene evidence.

The outline must preserve this format's narrator role, emotional engine, beat order, and proof weight. Each beat should name what gets said, what concrete scene or evidence carries it, where the mechanism enters, and where the product earns its first product mention.

Recovery proof, result timelines, victory scenes, and CTA confidence enter after the product or routine discovery has happened. The final script should be planned as feed-native LFS: one thought per paragraph, blank line between thoughts, `========` dividers between sections.

BEAT SEQUENCE:
1. Hook — "Dear [specific identity]..." The identity qualifier is the scroll-stop. It must be narrow enough that the right person thinks "that's me" and everyone else scrolls past.
2. The shared wound — the narrator addresses the reader's pain directly, in second person. "You've been..." / "You know that feeling when..." The narrator knows because they WERE the reader.
3. The validation — permission before mechanism. "You haven't failed. You've been aimed at the wrong target." The letter format earns the longest validation beat because the direct address creates intimacy.
4. The turn — "I want to tell you what I found." Transition from "I see you" to "I found something." Still addressed TO the reader.
5. The mechanism — delivered as something the narrator is EXPLAINING to the reader personally. Conversational, warm. "Here's what nobody told either of us." Second person weaves in: "your body" / "your [symptom]."
6. The search — compressed. The letter format doesn't need a long search because the intimacy was built through direct address, not through the hunt.
7. The product — "I want to share what worked for me." Personal recommendation to someone the narrator cares about.
8. The transformation — second person future-pacing. "You'll notice..." / "The morning you..." The reader sees THEMSELVES in the result, not the narrator.
9. CTA — the softest close of any format. "If you're ready..." / "Whenever you're ready..." The letter gives permission, not urgency.

WEIGHT: 30% on beats 1-3 (the address + shared wound + validation). The letter lives on intimacy. If the reader doesn't feel personally spoken to by beat 3, the format failed.

WHAT MAKES THIS DIFFERENT: Second person throughout. The narrator is talking TO the reader, not telling a story the reader overhears. Every beat is "you" not "I." The mechanism is a gift being shared, not a discovery being reported.
