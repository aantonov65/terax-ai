FORMAT: CHALLENGE / EXPERIMENT
Time-bound test with documented results. "I tried X for 30/60/90 days."


LFS V4.1 OUTLINE USE:
This file is the selected native shape for the outline writer. Treat the format as the container, then fill every beat with the source swipe pressure curve, product mechanism, and buyer-facing scene evidence.

The outline must preserve this format's narrator role, emotional engine, beat order, and proof weight. Each beat should name what gets said, what concrete scene or evidence carries it, where the mechanism enters, and where the product earns its first product mention.

Recovery proof, result timelines, victory scenes, and CTA confidence enter after the product or routine discovery has happened. The final script should be planned as feed-native LFS: one thought per paragraph, blank line between thoughts, `========` dividers between sections.

BEAT SEQUENCE:
1. Hook — the challenge statement. "I tried [specific thing] for [timeframe]. Here's what happened." The scroll-stop is the experiment: the reader wants to see the results.
2. The setup — why the narrator ran the experiment. What problem they had, what they'd already tried, why they were skeptical enough to frame this as a test rather than a commitment.
3. The rules — what specifically they did. How much, how often, what they changed, what they kept the same. Scientific framing: one variable changed, everything else controlled.
4. Week 1-2 — first observations. Honest. Include the doubt: "I almost quit at day 8." Small signals that something might be changing.
5. Week 3-4 — the shift. Measurable change. The narrator starts tracking something specific (a number, a behavior, a visual change). The skepticism cracks.
6. Month 2-3 — the result. Clear, documented. Before vs after. The experiment has a verdict.
7. The mechanism — brief. Explained AFTER the results, as the "why it worked" retrospective. The results already did the convincing. The mechanism is context, not argument.
8. CTA — "try it yourself" tone. The experiment frame makes the purchase feel like the reader running their own test. Risk-free because of the guarantee: "if it doesn't work for you, you'll know in [timeframe]."

WEIGHT: 50% on beats 4-6 (the documented progression). The challenge format is a PROOF format. The reader is convinced by watching the experiment unfold over time. If the progression is vague or the results appear too fast, the format loses credibility.

WHAT MAKES THIS DIFFERENT: The narrator is a tester, not a believer. Skepticism is the emotional register throughout — it gradually erodes as results accumulate. The reader trusts the format because the narrator approached it the way THEY would: with doubt, measurements, and a willingness to quit if it didn't work.
