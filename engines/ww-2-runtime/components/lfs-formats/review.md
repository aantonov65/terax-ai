FORMAT: REVIEW / VERDICT
Analytical evaluation. "Here's my honest take after [timeframe]."


LFS V4.1 OUTLINE USE:
This file is the selected native shape for the outline writer. Treat the format as the container, then fill every beat with the source swipe pressure curve, product mechanism, and buyer-facing scene evidence.

The outline must preserve this format's narrator role, emotional engine, beat order, and proof weight. Each beat should name what gets said, what concrete scene or evidence carries it, where the mechanism enters, and where the product earns its first product mention.

Recovery proof, result timelines, victory scenes, and CTA confidence enter after the product or routine discovery has happened. The final script should be planned as feed-native LFS: one thought per paragraph, blank line between thoughts, `========` dividers between sections.

BEAT SEQUENCE:
1. Hook — the verdict timeline. "I've been using [product/approach] for [timeframe]. Here's my honest review." The scroll-stop is the promise of genuine evaluation, not promotion.
2. The context — why the narrator tried it. What they were dealing with, what they'd tried before. Compressed — the review format front-loads the evaluation, not the backstory.
3. First impressions — what happened in the first week. Be specific. Include the doubts. "The first few days I thought it was another waste of money."
4. What worked — specific, measurable results. Numbers, timelines, behavioral changes. The narrator is REPORTING, not promoting.
5. What didn't work — honesty beat. Something that wasn't perfect. A limitation, a side note, something that took longer than expected. This is what makes the review feel real. A review with zero criticism reads as an ad.
6. The mechanism — WHY it worked, explained in retrospect. The narrator now understands the science behind their results. Brief.
7. The comparison — how it stacks against what they tried before. Price comparison. Result comparison. Effort comparison. Specific numbers.
8. The verdict — clear recommendation with conditions. "If you're dealing with [specific situation], this is the best [price/result ratio] I've found. If you're [different situation], [caveat]."
9. CTA — reviewer tone. "I'd give it [rating]. Link below if you want to try it." Matter-of-fact.

WEIGHT: 40% on beats 4-5 (what worked + what didn't). The review format is defined by BALANCED HONESTY. If everything is positive, the reader assumes it's an ad. The "what didn't work" beat is mandatory — it's what makes the "what worked" beats believable.

WHAT MAKES THIS DIFFERENT: The narrator is a reviewer, not a believer. The emotional register is analytical and measured. Trust is built through balance — praising AND criticizing. The reader treats this as a decision tool, not a story. The CTA is the verdict of an evaluation, not the end of a journey.
