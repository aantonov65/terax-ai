FORMAT: SECRET / INSIDER
The narrator has access to information the reader wasn't supposed to have.


LFS V4.1 OUTLINE USE:
This file is the selected native shape for the outline writer. Treat the format as the container, then fill every beat with the source swipe pressure curve, product mechanism, and buyer-facing scene evidence.

The outline must preserve this format's narrator role, emotional engine, beat order, and proof weight. Each beat should name what gets said, what concrete scene or evidence carries it, where the mechanism enters, and where the product earns its first product mention.

Recovery proof, result timelines, victory scenes, and CTA confidence enter after the product or routine discovery has happened. The final script should be planned as feed-native LFS: one thought per paragraph, blank line between thoughts, `========` dividers between sections.

BEAT SEQUENCE:
1. Hook — the insider credential + the withheld information. "My [relation] works in [industry]. Here's what they told me they actually use for [problem]." The scroll-stop is the access.
2. The narrator's problem — compressed. They had the same problem as the reader. 2-3 sentences.
3. The insider connection — how the narrator got access. A conversation, a visit, seeing something they weren't supposed to see. Specific scene: a barbecue, a phone call, an offhand comment.
4. The withheld information — what the insider revealed. Framed as something professionals know but don't share with consumers. Follow the money: why they keep it quiet.
5. The mechanism — arrives as the insider's knowledge translated for the reader. The narrator explains what the professional told them in plain language.
6. The gap — "So I asked them: can I buy this?" The insider says no — it's professional-only, proprietary, not available retail. Or: the retail versions are diluted, wrong concentration, missing key ingredients.
7. The search — the narrator hunts for a consumer version of what the professional uses. Specific dead ends. The product arrives as the one thing that matches the insider's formula.
8. The result — validation from the insider. "My [relation] looked at the ingredients and said 'yeah, that's basically what we use.'"
9. CTA — "sharing a cheat code with a friend" tone. The narrator is passing along insider access.

WEIGHT: 35% on beats 3-5 (the insider connection + revelation + mechanism). The format lives on the feeling of ACCESS. If the insider reveal feels like a product feature list, the format failed. It must feel like something the reader wasn't supposed to hear.

WHAT MAKES THIS DIFFERENT: The narrator is a conduit, not the expert. The authority comes from the SOURCE (the insider), not from the narrator's personal experience. The mechanism is framed as leaked professional knowledge, not as a discovery or a confession.
