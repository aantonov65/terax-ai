FORMAT: EAVESDROP
Access to something you weren't supposed to hear. The reader is a fly on the wall.


LFS V4.1 OUTLINE USE:
This file is the selected native shape for the outline writer. Treat the format as the container, then fill every beat with the source swipe pressure curve, product mechanism, and buyer-facing scene evidence.

The outline must preserve this format's narrator role, emotional engine, beat order, and proof weight. Each beat should name what gets said, what concrete scene or evidence carries it, where the mechanism enters, and where the product earns its first product mention.

Recovery proof, result timelines, victory scenes, and CTA confidence enter after the product or routine discovery has happened. The final script should be planned as feed-native LFS: one thought per paragraph, blank line between thoughts, `========` dividers between sections.

BEAT SEQUENCE:
1. Hook — the setup. The narrator overheard, saw, or accessed something not meant for them. "I was sitting in the waiting room when I heard [professional] say something to [another professional] through the wall." The scroll-stop is the forbidden access.
2. The scene — set the physical space. Specific enough that the reader is THERE. Waiting room, parking lot, conference, dinner party. The narrator wasn't supposed to hear this.
3. What was said — the overheard information. Presented as close to verbatim as possible. Short, punchy lines of dialogue or observation. The mechanism is embedded in what was overheard.
4. What it meant — the narrator processes what they heard. Connects it to their own problem. "That's when I realized what [my problem] actually was."
5. The investigation — the narrator follows up on what they overheard. Verifies it. Discovers more. The overheard moment was the key that unlocked a door.
6. The mechanism — framed as the truth behind the overheard conversation. What the professionals know and discuss privately but don't tell consumers.
7. The product — found through the investigation that the eavesdrop triggered.
8. The result — the narrator applied what they learned. It worked.
9. CTA — "you weren't supposed to hear this either" tone. Conspiratorial, intimate.

WEIGHT: 35% on beats 1-3 (the setup + scene + overheard content). The format lives on VOYEURISM. The reader must feel like they're accessing private information. If the overheard conversation sounds like a product pitch, the format fails. It must sound like a real conversation between professionals who don't know anyone is listening.

WHAT MAKES THIS DIFFERENT: The narrator is an accidental spy. The emotional register is "I can't believe I heard this." The mechanism is OVERHEARD, not discovered or researched. The authority comes from the SOURCE of the conversation, not from the narrator. Maximum intimacy — the reader feels like they're reading someone's private journal.
