FORMAT: APOLOGY
"I'm sorry I recommended X." Remorse as the engine.


LFS V4.1 OUTLINE USE:
This file is the selected native shape for the outline writer. Treat the format as the container, then fill every beat with the source swipe pressure curve, product mechanism, and buyer-facing scene evidence.

The outline must preserve this format's narrator role, emotional engine, beat order, and proof weight. Each beat should name what gets said, what concrete scene or evidence carries it, where the mechanism enters, and where the product earns its first product mention.

Recovery proof, result timelines, victory scenes, and CTA confidence enter after the product or routine discovery has happened. The final script should be planned as feed-native LFS: one thought per paragraph, blank line between thoughts, `========` dividers between sections.

BEAT SEQUENCE:
1. Hook — the apology upfront. "I owe you an apology. I recommended [specific thing] and I was wrong." The scroll-stop is the admission of guilt from someone who should have known better.
2. What they recommended — the specific product, approach, or advice they gave. Who they gave it to. The relationship that makes the apology personal.
3. What happened — the consequence. The recommendation didn't just fail — it made things worse. Specific damage: money wasted, time lost, condition worsened, trust broken.
4. Why they were wrong — the narrator explains what they didn't understand at the time. The mechanism enters here as the MISSING KNOWLEDGE that made their recommendation wrong. They're not bad — they were incomplete.
5. What they should have said — the corrected recommendation. The mechanism is framed as what the narrator WISHES they'd known. Each point of the old recommendation is paired with what should have been said instead.
6. The product — what the narrator would recommend NOW. Arrives with the weight of someone who got it wrong once and can't afford to get it wrong again.
7. The result — what happened when the person they apologized to tried the corrected approach. Specific. The apology is REDEEMED.
8. CTA — humble, earnest. "I got this wrong. I'm trying to get it right now." The narrator is making amends through the recommendation.

WEIGHT: 35% on beats 1-3 (the apology + consequence). The format lives on GENUINE REMORSE. If the apology feels performative or the narrator isn't really accountable, the format collapses into a regular product recommendation. The reader must believe the narrator feels personally responsible.

WHAT MAKES THIS DIFFERENT: The narrator is guilty, not suffering. The emotional register is remorse and accountability. Trust is built through the admission of being wrong — the reader thinks "if they're willing to admit this, the new recommendation must be real." The mechanism is the CORRECTION of a genuine mistake.
