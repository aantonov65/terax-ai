FORMAT: CONFESSION FROM THE OTHER SIDE
A doctor, spouse, friend, or professional confessing what they should have done differently. Guilt from someone who COULD have helped.


LFS V4.1 OUTLINE USE:
This file is the selected native shape for the outline writer. Treat the format as the container, then fill every beat with the source swipe pressure curve, product mechanism, and buyer-facing scene evidence.

The outline must preserve this format's narrator role, emotional engine, beat order, and proof weight. Each beat should name what gets said, what concrete scene or evidence carries it, where the mechanism enters, and where the product earns its first product mention.

Recovery proof, result timelines, victory scenes, and CTA confidence enter after the product or routine discovery has happened. The final script should be planned as feed-native LFS: one thought per paragraph, blank line between thoughts, `========` dividers between sections.

BEAT SEQUENCE:
1. Hook — the confession from the other perspective. "I'm a [professional/relation]. I watched [someone] suffer with [problem] for [timeframe]. I could have helped sooner. I didn't."
2. What they saw — the other person's suffering, observed from the outside. Specific moments the narrator witnessed but didn't intervene in. The guilt is in the SEEING.
3. Why they didn't act — professional protocol, ignorance, assumptions, following the standard playbook. The narrator explains what kept them silent.
4. What changed — a specific moment where the narrator realized the standard approach was wrong. A patient who got worse, a conversation that shifted their thinking, a study they read.
5. The mechanism — what the narrator now understands that they didn't before. Framed as professional awakening. The mechanism has clinical weight because it changed an EXPERT'S mind.
6. What they did — the narrator applied the new understanding. Specific: recommended something different, changed their practice, told the person they'd been watching suffer.
7. The result — what happened to the person they should have helped sooner. The redemption beat. The guilt transforms into relief.
8. CTA — "I should have said this sooner." The narrator is confessing publicly to help others they can't reach individually.

WEIGHT: 35% on beats 1-3 (the confession + witnessing + guilt). The format lives on GUILT FROM POWER. The narrator had the ability or knowledge to help and didn't. If the narrator is just another sufferer, this is a regular confession, not a confession from the other side.

WHAT MAKES THIS DIFFERENT: The narrator is NOT the person with the problem. They're the person who WATCHED. The emotional register is guilt and accountability, not pain. The mechanism is validated by someone who changed their professional mind — maximum credibility.
