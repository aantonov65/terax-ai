FORMAT: CONFESSION
Raw first-person vulnerability. The narrator's wound IS the reader's wound.


LFS V4.1 OUTLINE USE:
This file is the selected native shape for the outline writer. Treat the format as the container, then fill every beat with the source swipe pressure curve, product mechanism, and buyer-facing scene evidence.

The outline must preserve this format's narrator role, emotional engine, beat order, and proof weight. Each beat should name what gets said, what concrete scene or evidence carries it, where the mechanism enters, and where the product earns its first product mention.

Recovery proof, result timelines, victory scenes, and CTA confidence enter after the product or routine discovery has happened. The final script should be planned as feed-native LFS: one thought per paragraph, blank line between thoughts, `========` dividers between sections.

BEAT SEQUENCE:
1. Hook — one specific moment of pain. Not a summary of the problem. A single scene: a time, a place, a sensory detail. The reader sees it before they understand what the ad is about.
2. The deeper wound — what the visible problem cost them emotionally or socially. Not more symptoms — what the symptoms DID to their life. A relationship moment, a social moment, a private moment.
3. The search — what they tried, framed as hope followed by disappointment. Each attempt was an act of faith. Each failure was personal. 2-3 failed solutions, each with emotional weight, not just biological dismantling.
4. The turn — a specific moment where something shifted. A conversation, a discovery, a late-night search that landed somewhere new. This is where the mechanism enters, but through the narrator's experience of finding it — not as an explanation.
5. The mechanism — brief and warm. Bro-science level. The narrator explains it the way they understood it, not the way a doctor would. Analogies from their life, not from a textbook.
6. The product — arrives naturally from the search. The narrator didn't go looking for THIS product. They went looking for what the mechanism told them they needed, and this is what they found.
7. The transformation — slow build through four levels. The confession format earns the longest transformation section because the reader has been in the wound with the narrator. Each level of recovery mirrors a specific pain moment from beats 1-2.
8. CTA — the most intimate close. Permission beat first. The narrator shares what they did, not what the reader should do. "If you're where I was..."

WEIGHT: 30% on beats 1-2 (the wound), 30% on beat 7 (the transformation). The confession is bookended by emotional weight. The mechanism is compressed in the middle — trust was built through vulnerability, not credentials.

WHAT MAKES THIS DIFFERENT: The narrator is not explaining or investigating or warning. They're confessing. The emotional register is tender, not urgent. The reader's response is "that's me" not "I didn't know that." Pain and transformation carry the format — mechanism is the bridge between them, not the centerpiece.
