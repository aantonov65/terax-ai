FORMAT: ORIGIN STORY
How the solution was discovered or created. The product has a backstory worth telling.


LFS V4.1 OUTLINE USE:
This file is the selected native shape for the outline writer. Treat the format as the container, then fill every beat with the source swipe pressure curve, product mechanism, and buyer-facing scene evidence.

The outline must preserve this format's narrator role, emotional engine, beat order, and proof weight. Each beat should name what gets said, what concrete scene or evidence carries it, where the mechanism enters, and where the product earns its first product mention.

Recovery proof, result timelines, victory scenes, and CTA confidence enter after the product or routine discovery has happened. The final script should be planned as feed-native LFS: one thought per paragraph, blank line between thoughts, `========` dividers between sections.

BEAT SEQUENCE:
1. Hook — the origin moment. A specific scene where the discovery began. "A researcher in [place] noticed something nobody else was looking at." Or: "It started with a question nobody was asking."
2. The problem landscape — what everyone was doing and why it wasn't working. The narrator surveys the field from above, not from personal suffering.
3. The question — what made someone look at the problem differently. A specific observation, anomaly, or frustration that triggered the investigation.
4. The discovery — step by step. The mechanism unfolds as a STORY, not as an explanation. Each step of the discovery creates an "aha" moment. The reader follows the detective work.
5. The obstacle — why the discovery wasn't immediately available. Regulatory barriers, industry resistance, cost, or simply nobody connecting the dots. Creates the gap between "we know the answer" and "you can actually use it."
6. The bridge — how the discovery became a product. Who made it available, how they maintained the quality/concentration, why it works as a consumer product when professional versions exist.
7. The narrator's connection — how the narrator found the product and what happened. Compressed personal story layered onto the origin.
8. CTA — "you're hearing about this because someone finally made it available" tone. The product is the end of a long origin story.

WEIGHT: 40% on beats 3-5 (the question + discovery + obstacle). The origin story format is a NARRATIVE about the solution's creation. If it reads like a "founded in 2019" brand story, the format failed. It must feel like following a detective through a case.

WHAT MAKES THIS DIFFERENT: The narrator is a storyteller, not a sufferer. The problem is presented from the outside — the narrator may or may not have the condition. The emotional pull is CURIOSITY about the discovery, not empathy with the pain. The mechanism IS the plot.
