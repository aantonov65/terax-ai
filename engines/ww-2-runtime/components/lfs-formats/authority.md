FORMAT: AUTHORITY
Expert-led. Credibility first, emotion second.


LFS V4.1 OUTLINE USE:
This file is the selected native shape for the outline writer. Treat the format as the container, then fill every beat with the source swipe pressure curve, product mechanism, and buyer-facing scene evidence.

The outline must preserve this format's narrator role, emotional engine, beat order, and proof weight. Each beat should name what gets said, what concrete scene or evidence carries it, where the mechanism enters, and where the product earns its first product mention.

Recovery proof, result timelines, victory scenes, and CTA confidence enter after the product or routine discovery has happened. The final script should be planned as feed-native LFS: one thought per paragraph, blank line between thoughts, `========` dividers between sections.

BEAT SEQUENCE:
1. Hook — the credential in the first sentence. "[N] years in [field]" or "[N] patients with this exact problem." The scroll-stop is the expertise, not the pain.
2. The pattern — what the expert sees over and over. "Every week someone comes to me with [problem]. They've already tried [list]. And they all have the same thing in common." The expert has seen this thousands of times.
3. What everyone gets wrong — the expert dismantles common approaches clinically. Not with emotion — with evidence. Each one gets a specific mechanical reason for failure. The tone is "this is what the data shows," not "this is what I felt."
4. What the expert knows — the mechanism, delivered with authority. References: studies, patient counts, clinical observations. The expert doesn't discover the mechanism — they've KNOWN it. They're finally explaining it publicly.
5. Why they're speaking up — the expert's motivation. Frustration with the industry, guilt about what they've seen, wanting to help outside the clinical setting. This is the ONE emotional beat — the expert is human.
6. The product — the expert endorses it specifically. "This is the closest consumer product to what I'd recommend clinically." The endorsement carries because the credential was established early.
7. The result — framed as clinical observation, not personal testimony. "Patients who [did this] saw [specific result] in [timeframe]. N=X."
8. CTA — authoritative but warm. "As a [professional], I'd recommend trying this for 90 days." The expert gives a professional recommendation, not a sales pitch.

WEIGHT: 35% on beats 2-4 (the pattern + dismantling + mechanism). The authority format is defined by CLINICAL WEIGHT. If the expert sounds like a marketer, the format failed. Every claim must feel evidence-backed, not experiential.

WHAT MAKES THIS DIFFERENT: Third person emotional distance. The expert doesn't have the problem — they TREAT the problem. Pain is observed, not felt. The mechanism gets the most detailed, evidence-dense treatment of any format because the expert has the credibility to go deep without losing the reader.
