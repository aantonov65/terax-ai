FORMAT: PREDICTION / FUTURE-CAST
Slow zoom into a future the reader doesn't want. Then the exit.


LFS V4.1 OUTLINE USE:
This file is the selected native shape for the outline writer. Treat the format as the container, then fill every beat with the source swipe pressure curve, product mechanism, and buyer-facing scene evidence.

The outline must preserve this format's narrator role, emotional engine, beat order, and proof weight. Each beat should name what gets said, what concrete scene or evidence carries it, where the mechanism enters, and where the product earns its first product mention.

Recovery proof, result timelines, victory scenes, and CTA confidence enter after the product or routine discovery has happened. The final script should be planned as feed-native LFS: one thought per paragraph, blank line between thoughts, `========` dividers between sections.

BEAT SEQUENCE:
1. Hook — a present-moment observation that implies a trajectory. "That [symptom] you noticed [timeframe] ago? It's not going to stop on its own. Here's what happens next if nothing changes."
2. Month 3 — specific, believable progression. What gets slightly worse. Named symptoms, named behaviors that shift.
3. Month 6 — escalation. The problem compounds. New symptoms appear that are downstream of the original. The reader sees a pattern.
4. Year 1 — the full picture. Social consequences, financial costs, emotional weight. The future is specific and lived-in, not abstract doom.
5. The pivot — "But that's only if nothing changes. Something can change." The prediction stops. The narrator offers the exit.
6. The mechanism — framed as the thing that INTERRUPTS the trajectory. The mechanism doesn't just fix the current symptom — it prevents the predicted future.
7. The product — compressed. The prediction did the emotional work. The product is the interrupt button.
8. The result — the narrator's alternative timeline. "That was the prediction for me [timeframe] ago. Here's where I am instead." Counter-future.
9. CTA — "you're standing at the fork" tone. Two timelines: the prediction, or the alternative. The reader chooses.

WEIGHT: 40% on beats 2-4 (the prediction timeline). The format lives on SPECIFIC, BELIEVABLE escalation. If the future feels exaggerated or fear-mongering, the format fails. Each stage must feel like something the reader can verify against their own experience — "yeah, that IS getting worse."

WHAT MAKES THIS DIFFERENT: The narrator is a prophet, not a sufferer. The emotional register is serious, not sad. The pain isn't in the past — it's in the FUTURE. The reader is motivated by what hasn't happened yet, not what already did. Urgency comes from trajectory, not from current suffering.
