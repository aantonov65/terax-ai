FORMAT: VERSUS / COMPARISON
Two options compared side by side. One wins. The reader knows which before the narrator says it.


LFS V4.1 OUTLINE USE:
This file is the selected native shape for the outline writer. Treat the format as the container, then fill every beat with the source swipe pressure curve, product mechanism, and buyer-facing scene evidence.

The outline must preserve this format's narrator role, emotional engine, beat order, and proof weight. Each beat should name what gets said, what concrete scene or evidence carries it, where the mechanism enters, and where the product earns its first product mention.

Recovery proof, result timelines, victory scenes, and CTA confidence enter after the product or routine discovery has happened. The final script should be planned as feed-native LFS: one thought per paragraph, blank line between thoughts, `========` dividers between sections.

BEAT SEQUENCE:
1. Hook — the comparison framed as a question. "What's the difference between [person who still suffers] and [person who solved it]? It's not [obvious thing]. It's not [second obvious thing]." The scroll-stop is the mystery of the differential.
2. Option A — the conventional approach. Name it, price it, explain what it does. Be fair. Don't strawman. The reader probably uses Option A or is considering it.
3. Option A's ceiling — the structural limitation. Why Option A can only take you to 20-30%. Specific, mechanical. "Option A addresses [one pathway]. The problem runs on [two pathways]."
4. Option B — the mechanism approach. What it does differently. Framed as the answer to Option A's ceiling, not as a competing product.
5. The comparison — side by side. Same problem, same person, two different outcomes. Timeline A vs Timeline B. Cost A vs Cost B. Result A vs Result B.
6. The narrator's experience — "I used Option A for [timeframe]. Then I switched." Personal transition from A to B.
7. The result — the differential made visible. What changed when they switched. Specific markers.
8. CTA — "I'm not saying Option A is bad. I'm saying it has a ceiling." Respectful of the reader's current choice. The comparison did the selling.

WEIGHT: 35% on beats 2-5 (the comparison). The format lives on FAIRNESS. If Option A is strawmanned, the reader distrusts the comparison and the format fails. Option A must be presented accurately — then defeated by structural logic, not bias.

WHAT MAKES THIS DIFFERENT: The narrator is analytical, not emotional. The format is a decision tool. The reader feels like they're making an informed choice, not being sold to. The mechanism is embedded in the comparison — it's the REASON Option B wins, not a separate teaching section.
