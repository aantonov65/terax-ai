FORMAT: RANT
Channeled frustration as the engine. The narrator says what the reader is thinking.


LFS V4.1 OUTLINE USE:
This file is the selected native shape for the outline writer. Treat the format as the container, then fill every beat with the source swipe pressure curve, product mechanism, and buyer-facing scene evidence.

The outline must preserve this format's narrator role, emotional engine, beat order, and proof weight. Each beat should name what gets said, what concrete scene or evidence carries it, where the mechanism enters, and where the product earns its first product mention.

Recovery proof, result timelines, victory scenes, and CTA confidence enter after the product or routine discovery has happened. The final script should be planned as feed-native LFS: one thought per paragraph, blank line between thoughts, `========` dividers between sections.

BEAT SEQUENCE:
1. Hook — raw frustration, controlled. "I'm going to say what every [identity] with [problem] is thinking." The scroll-stop is the permission to be angry.
2. The frustration stack — 3-4 specific things that are infuriating about the problem. Not abstract anger — named products, named prices, named failures. Each one escalates. The narrator channels the reader's accumulated frustration.
3. The system — who benefits from this. The rant pivots from personal frustration to systemic indictment. "And the worst part? They KNOW it doesn't work. They sell it anyway."
4. The question — "So what actually works?" The rant earns the right to ask because the narrator proved they understand the problem deeply enough to reject everything that doesn't work.
5. The mechanism — brief. The rant reader doesn't want a lecture. They want the answer. The mechanism is compressed to its essential logic: what's different and why.
6. The product — "I almost didn't try it." The rant narrator is skeptical by nature. The product must overcome the narrator's own anger to earn its place.
7. The result — vindication. The anger was justified. The solution was simple. Both are true. "You know what's satisfying? [Specific result]."
8. CTA — still a little heated. "If you're tired of [the thing they ranted about], try this." The rant narrator doesn't soften fully. The edge stays.

WEIGHT: 40% on beats 2-3 (the frustration stack + system indictment). The rant format is fueled by SHARED anger. If the frustration feels generic or performative, the format failed. Each frustration must be something the reader has personally experienced and been unable to articulate.

WHAT MAKES THIS DIFFERENT: The narrator is angry, not sad. The emotional register is heat, not vulnerability. Trust is built through shared rage — "finally someone said it." The mechanism is compressed because the rant reader is impatient. They want the answer, not the education. The CTA keeps its edge — no soft landing, no permission beat.
