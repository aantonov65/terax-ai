FORMAT: FOLLOW-UP / UPDATE
Implies a previous chapter. The reader enters mid-story with instant credibility.


LFS V4.1 OUTLINE USE:
This file is the selected native shape for the outline writer. Treat the format as the container, then fill every beat with the source swipe pressure curve, product mechanism, and buyer-facing scene evidence.

The outline must preserve this format's narrator role, emotional engine, beat order, and proof weight. Each beat should name what gets said, what concrete scene or evidence carries it, where the mechanism enters, and where the product earns its first product mention.

Recovery proof, result timelines, victory scenes, and CTA confidence enter after the product or routine discovery has happened. The final script should be planned as feed-native LFS: one thought per paragraph, blank line between thoughts, `========` dividers between sections.

BEAT SEQUENCE:
1. Hook — the update headline. "Quick update: [timeframe] since I [did the thing], [specific result]." Or: "A few months ago I posted about my [problem]. Here's what happened since." The scroll-stop is the implied history.
2. The backstory — compressed recap for people who didn't see the "original." 3-4 sentences covering the problem, what failed, how bad it got. Written as a reminder, not a full retelling.
3. What changed — the turning point. What the narrator tried differently. The mechanism enters here, compressed, as context for the update — not as a teaching moment.
4. The timeline — the core of the format. Week by week or month by month. Specific observations. What changed first, what changed next, what surprised them. Behavioral markers: "stopped doing X," "started doing Y."
5. Where they are now — the current state. Specific, present-tense. "This morning I [did something normal without thinking about it]."
6. What they'd do differently — retrospective advice. "If I'd known [this] sooner, I would have [that]." One or two sentences of earned wisdom.
7. CTA — "figured I'd share in case someone needs it" tone. The follow-up narrator isn't selling. They're documenting. The link is shared as an afterthought, not a pitch.

WEIGHT: 40% on beat 4 (the timeline). The follow-up format is proof-forward. The reader is convinced by the documented progression, not by the argument or the emotion. If the timeline is vague ("it got better over time"), the format failed. Specific weeks, specific observations.

WHAT MAKES THIS DIFFERENT: The narrator is already on the other side. There's no active pain, no active search. The emotional register is calm, retrospective, matter-of-fact. Trust is built through documented results over time, not through vulnerability or investigation. The implied "previous post" creates instant authority without credentials.
