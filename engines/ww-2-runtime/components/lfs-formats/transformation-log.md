FORMAT: TRANSFORMATION LOG
Pure chronological documentation. Timestamps and observations. Zero narrative arc.


LFS V4.1 OUTLINE USE:
This file is the selected native shape for the outline writer. Treat the format as the container, then fill every beat with the source swipe pressure curve, product mechanism, and buyer-facing scene evidence.

The outline must preserve this format's narrator role, emotional engine, beat order, and proof weight. Each beat should name what gets said, what concrete scene or evidence carries it, where the mechanism enters, and where the product earns its first product mention.

Recovery proof, result timelines, victory scenes, and CTA confidence enter after the product or routine discovery has happened. The final script should be planned as feed-native LFS: one thought per paragraph, blank line between thoughts, `========` dividers between sections.

BEAT SEQUENCE:
1. Hook — the start date and starting condition. "Day 1. [Specific measurement or observation]." The scroll-stop is the diary format — it looks different from every other ad.
2. Days 1-7 — honest early observations. Mostly nothing. "Day 3. No change. Starting to wonder if I wasted my money." The honesty builds credibility.
3. Days 8-14 — first signals. Small, specific. The narrator notes them without celebration. "Day 11. Noticed [specific small change]. Could be coincidence."
4. Weeks 3-4 — the shift becomes undeniable. Multiple observations. Others start noticing. "Day 22. [Person] asked what I changed."
5. Month 2 — sustained results. The log entries get shorter because there's less to report — the problem is fading. "Day 45. Forgot to check."
6. Month 3 — the log stops because the problem is gone. "Day 90. Realized I haven't made an entry in two weeks. Nothing to report. That IS the entry."
7. Retrospective — one paragraph stepping back from the log. What the narrator understands now about the mechanism. Brief.
8. CTA — "Day 1 is whenever you decide." The log format makes the purchase feel like starting your own experiment.

WEIGHT: 60% on beats 2-6 (the log entries). The format IS the log. If the entries feel written in retrospect rather than in real-time, the format fails. Each entry must feel like a dated observation, not a narrative beat.

WHAT MAKES THIS DIFFERENT: Zero narrative voice. The narrator is a recorder, not a storyteller. No emotional language — just observations. Trust is built through the ACCUMULATION of small, dated entries. The format weaponizes monotony: the boring middle entries make the breakthrough entries hit harder.
