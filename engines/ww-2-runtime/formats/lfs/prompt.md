<output_structure>

Write one continuous first-person confession. 11 sections flowing into each other, separated by ======== dividers. Write approximately 1,400-1,600 words.

Section 1 — HOOK (20-50 words) ← {a_point} driven
Maximum 2-3 sentences. The scroll-stopper.

Rules:
- Start with the PUNCH, not the wind-up. No atmosphere. No scene-setting. No "I remember the night when..."
- The hook either INVERTS expectations (state something mundane, reveal why it's extraordinary) or SHOCKS with specificity (two numbers that shouldn't exist together, one humiliating detail).

Your A-point determines the entry angle:
- A1 (Physical): the body sensation, the sound, the specific object that broke them
- A2 (Timing): the age, the life stage, the exact moment it started
- A3 (Social): the public humiliation in one sentence

CONTRASTIVE — study the delta:

BAD: "I never thought I'd be the kind of person who dreads looking in the mirror. But there I was, standing in my bathroom at 2am, tears streaming down my face, wondering how everything had gotten so bad."
→ Literary. Scene-setting. 4 sentences when you need 2. Claude's default mode. DO NOT WRITE THIS.

GOOD: "I washed my hair this morning and didn't think about it. If you've been where I was eighteen months ago, you know how big that is."
→ Normalcy inversion. Quiet. Specific. The reader who GETS it stops scrolling.

GOOD: "Knee replacement surgery: $58,000. What three surgeons missed: $27."
→ Price shock. Two numbers. Done.

GOOD: "I couldn't open a pickle jar anymore. My 9-year-old grandson opened it without looking up from his iPad."
→ Specific humiliation. Two sentences. No feelings described — the scene IS the feeling.

Section 2 — PAIN SETUP (180-220 words) ← {a_point} driven
When it started. How it escalated. Stay in the A-point lane.

Section 3 — SYMPTOM BREAKDOWN (220-280 words) ← {a_point} driven
Visceral, embarrassing details. What they see, feel, smell, hear. What they hide from others.

Section 4 — FAILED SOLUTIONS (180-220 words)
Everything they tried. Specific products, costs, timeframes. Dismissive doctor quotes. End on "Nothing worked."

Section 5 — ROCK BOTTOM (180-220 words) ← {a_point} driven
The specific scene where they broke. Cinematic: location, dialogue, physical sensations, the emotion they couldn't hold back anymore.

Section 6 — DISCOVERY + MECHANISM (300-400 words) ← {b_point} driven
Three parts, flowing naturally:

Part A — The mechanism revelation (150-200 words). Late-night research, a forum post, an article. They discover WHY their problem exists. Use the mechanism from research context VERBATIM. Frame through the B-point lens:
- B1: the biological "aha"
- B2: reclaiming identity
- B3: rage at the system that failed them

Part B — The search struggle (60-100 words). CRITICAL SECTION — do NOT skip this. Now they KNOW what's wrong, but finding the right solution is its own problem. The stuff that works is hard to find. Most products don't have the right form, or the right dose, or they cost a fortune from specialty labs. "You can't just walk into CVS and buy this." "The clinical-grade version runs $200/month." "Every supplement I found had the wrong form." This paragraph makes the product arrival feel EARNED — the narrator went through a search, not a sales funnel.

Part C — The product bridge (80-120 words). NOW they find {product_name}. The product enters as the END of a search, not the start of a pitch. "A friend sent me a link" / "I stumbled on this company" / "I almost missed it in a forum thread." Brief ingredient mapping — how each ingredient solves the specific mechanism:
{ingredients_text}

Price anchoring: what alternatives cost vs this. The {product_guarantee} guarantee framed as "worst case, I'm out nothing" — a safety net, not a sales bullet.

CONTRASTIVE — study the delta:

BAD: "That's when I found {product_name}. It contains [ingredient 1] for [benefit] and [ingredient 2] for [benefit]. Plus it has a {product_guarantee} money-back guarantee!"
→ Reads like a landing page. The narrator transforms from a person into a salesman mid-paragraph.

GOOD: "Once I understood this, I started looking for bioavailable silica. Most synthetic versions barely absorb. But horsetail extract has 70% absorption rates. And I needed something to support my exhausted adrenals so they'd stop stealing from my hair. I found Noor Healthy Hair Formula. Two ingredients that made sense: horsetail extract for bioavailable silica, and pantothenic acid for adrenal support. Replace what's missing and stop the theft. Simple. They had this 180-day guarantee where you could return even empty bottles. I figured worst case, I'm out nothing."
→ The narrator is still a person SEARCHING. The product is the answer to their search, not a pitch. The guarantee is risk removal, not a feature.

Section 7 — RECOVERY TIMELINE (120-150 words) ← {b_point} driven
4-6 checkpoints: Week 1, Week 2, Month 1, Month 2, etc. Frame through B-point lens.

Section 8 — VICTORY STATE (100-140 words) ← {b_point} driven
Life now. The transformation. What they can do again.

Section 9 — DIRECT READER ADDRESS (60-80 words)
"If you're reading this..." Speak directly to the reader. Validate their experience. Acknowledge the struggle. Give hope WITHOUT selling. This is a human moment — one person who's been there talking to another. NOT a CTA. NOT a pitch. Just connection.

Section 10 — CTA DANCE (100-150 words)
The CTA is a gradual wind-down, NOT an abrupt ask. Think of it as a friend casually sharing something that worked — not a salesman closing.

Three beats, flowing naturally:

Beat 1 — Personal share. "I got mine from their website — I'll drop the link below." Casual. Offhand. Like mentioning a restaurant you liked.

Beat 2 — Social proof echo. ONE other person who tried it. "My sister ordered it after seeing my results." "Three women in my group asked what I'm using." Specific person, specific result. This normalizes the action.

Beat 3 — Risk removal + the ask. The guarantee framed as "I figured worst case, I'm out nothing." Then the CTA text below:

{cta_instructions}

CONTRASTIVE:
BAD: [Victory section ends] → "Click LEARN MORE below to read an article about..." → P.S.
→ Whiplash. The narrator was a person, now they're a landing page. The tonal shift kills trust.

GOOD: [Victory section ends] → "I got mine from their website..." → "My sister tried it too..." → "If you're where I was, just read the article" → CTA text → P.S.
→ The CTA EMERGES from the story. The narrator never stops being a person.

Section 11 — P.S. CLOSE (30-60 words)
Callback to the hook. The story comes full circle. The same image or detail from Section 1, but transformed. End on the quiet victory, not on the sell.

"P.S. — [Callback to hook detail]. [How it's different now]. [One sentence of gratitude or peace — not a pitch]."

<voice_guide>

FORMATTING:
Write in plain text only. Use ======== between major sections. Use ALL CAPS sparingly for emphasis. Use ellipses for trailing thoughts. Use contractions always (I'm, don't, can't). Short paragraphs — 1 to 3 sentences. Emoji bullets only in the CTA section. One continuous personal story flowing from hook to close.

WORD ALLOCATION:
60% on PAIN (Sections 1-5). 25% on DISCOVERY (Sections 6-8). 15% on FREEDOM (Sections 9-11). If you run long, cut from the back half. The pain sections are sacred.

DOCTOR REFERENCES:
Use generic references: "my doctor," "the specialist," "the orthopedist," "researchers found," "a study showed." The winner examples may use specific doctor names — that was a mistake. Use generic references instead.
