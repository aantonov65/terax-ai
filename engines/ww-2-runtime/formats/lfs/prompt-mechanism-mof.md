Section 1 — HOOK (50-100 words)
The hook must create an OPEN LOOP that forces the reader to keep going. Use ONE of these proven structures — pick the one that fits the mechanism best:

**Contrarian Knockout**: Attack a common "healthy" practice. Negate it. Then tease the real answer.
  "Taking biotin for hair loss? That's like pouring water on a plant through a sealed pot. 88% of it never reaches your follicles. Here's what actually does."

**Percentage Paradox**: A specific, alarming stat that makes the reader think "wait, that's me."
  "76% of women taking hair supplements are absorbing less than 12% of what's in the pill. Your doctor has never tested for why."

**Visual Anomaly**: Make them LOOK at something. A scan, a test result. Then reveal what's missing.
  "Look at this blood panel. Everything says 'normal.' Now look at what's NOT on the panel. There's no test for the one thing that would explain everything."

**Geographic Mystery / Age Comparison**: Why does GROUP A not have this problem when GROUP B does?
  "Why don't women in Okinawa lose their hair after menopause despite eating half the protein American women do?"

**The Contradiction Discovery**: An authority finding that reverses conventional wisdom.
  "Johns Hopkins found that the #1 recommended hair supplement actually makes the problem worse in women over 55."

**The "What It's NOT" Opener** (Gundry format — extremely effective): List 4-5 popular solutions. Negate each. Then: "So what IS it?"
  "It's not biotin. Not collagen. Not iron. Not minoxidil. Not rosemary oil. 89% of women have tried at least three of those. None of them address the actual problem."

RULES FOR HOOKS:
- NEVER open with "There's a reason your X isn't working" — that is the blandest possible opening.
- NEVER open with "I want to talk about something" — nobody cares what you want to talk about.
- NEVER open with vague promises. Open with a SPECIFIC, FALSIFIABLE CLAIM.
- The hook must contain at least ONE specific number, percentage, or institution name in the first 2 sentences.
- The hook must NEGATE something the reader currently believes.
- Tease a reveal: "In a minute I'll show you the one test that catches this."
- Maximum 3 sentences before you drop the first SPECIFIC FACT.

Section 2 — WHY NOTHING WORKS (200-250 words)

Example: "Biotin. Everybody's first move. Makes sense — biotin IS important for keratin. But here's what the label doesn't tell you: biotin circulates in your blood. Use analogies from everyday life — NOT medical jargon. Garden hose. Sealed pot. Traffic jam. Locked door. These are the analogies that stick.

End this section with a BRIDGE that teases the mechanism: "Every single one of these fails for the same reason. And it's something that doesn't show up on any standard blood panel."
