<output_structure>

Write one continuous first-person story. No headers. No bold. No bullet formatting except in the CTA. Plain text exactly as it would appear in a Facebook post. 11 sections flowing as one unbroken narrative, separated by ======== dividers.

WORD COUNT: Minimum 1,400 words. Target 1,500 words. Do NOT write under 1,400. The pain sections (1-5) should be meaty and detailed — they carry 60% of the word count. If you're running short, expand the symptom breakdown and failed solutions with more visceral detail, more specific products, more doctor quotes.

Every paragraph earns the reader's permission to read the next one. If a paragraph doesn't create tension, curiosity, or identification — cut it.

Section 1 — HOOK (40-60 words) ← {a_point} driven
2-3 lines. Open mid-story. Drop the reader into a specific moment — no introduction, no setup, no "let me tell you about." ONE visceral detail that makes them feel seen instantly.
Your A-point determines the entry angle:
- A1 (Physical): the body sensation, the sound, the thing they see
- A2 (Timing): the age, the life stage, the "when did this start"
- A3 (Social): the public moment, the relationship, the humiliation

Section 2 — PAIN SETUP (180-220 words) ← {a_point} driven
When it started. How it escalated. Stay in the A-point lane.
Include one CONFESSION BEAT: a moment where the narrator admits she dismissed her own symptoms, told herself "it's just aging," mocked a friend for trying supplements, or ignored the problem. This self-admission builds trust before any claim is made.

Section 3 — SYMPTOM BREAKDOWN (220-280 words) ← {a_point} driven
Visceral, embarrassing details. What they see, feel, smell, hear. What they hide from others. Use sensory language — the specific thing they see in the mirror, the texture they feel when they touch their hair, the sound of hair hitting the bathroom floor.

Section 4 — FAILED SOLUTIONS (180-220 words)
Everything they tried. Specific products, dollar costs, timeframes. Dismissive doctor quotes in dialogue. End on "Nothing worked."
Use real brand names and prices: "$47/month on Nutrafol for 8 months — $376 total" is more believable than "expensive supplements." Specificity is proof.

Section 5 — ROCK BOTTOM (180-220 words) ← {a_point} driven
The specific scene where they broke. Cinematic: location, lighting, dialogue, physical sensations, the emotion they couldn't hold back anymore.

Section 6 — DISCOVERY + MECHANISM (280-350 words) ← {b_point} driven
Two parts:

Part A — The mechanism revelation. Late-night research, a forum post, an article. They discover WHY their problem exists. Use the mechanism from research context VERBATIM. Frame through the B-point lens:
- B1: the biological "aha"
- B2: reclaiming identity
- B3: rage at the system that failed them

Use TECHNICAL SPECIFICITY as proof. Pull specific numbers from the mechanism: dosages, absorption rates, biological processes. "440mg of bioavailable silica with 70% absorption" separates this from every vague supplement claim. Numbers function as evidence.

Part B — The product bridge. They find {product_name}. Explain how each ingredient solves the mechanism:
{ingredients_text}

Map each ingredient to the mechanism. The product must feel like the INEVITABLE answer to what they just learned. Weave in the {product_guarantee} guarantee naturally — not as a sales pitch, as reassurance ("180-day guarantee, so I figured worst case I'm out nothing").

Section 7 — RECOVERY TIMELINE (120-150 words) ← {b_point} driven
4-6 checkpoints: Week 2, Week 5, Week 7, Week 10, Month 3, Month 6. Use non-round numbers — they feel real. Frame through B-point lens.
Include one DELIBERATE UNDERSELL: "Is it perfect? No." or "I won't pretend I have my 25-year-old hair back." or "Am I back to what I had at 30? No. But..." One line of managed expectations does more for trust than ten lines of proof.

Section 8 — RIPPLE PROOF + VICTORY (120-160 words) ← {b_point} driven
After the narrator's results, someone close to them tries it too. Pick ONE — and vary it across scripts, do NOT always use "sister at Thanksgiving":
- Sister who noticed at a family event
- Friend from church or book club
- Neighbor who asked what changed
- Woman in the menopause support group
- Coworker who complimented her hair
- Hairdresser who told another client about it
Their result in 1-2 sentences — organic, embedded in the story. NOT a testimonial block.
Then: life now. The transformation. What they can do again.

Section 9 — DIRECT READER ADDRESS (60-80 words)
"If you're reading this..." Speak to them. Validate their experience. Give hope without hype.

<voice_guide>

TONE:
Write at a 6th-grade reading level. The reader should NEVER feel like they are reading an ad. This is a Facebook post from a real woman sharing something that helped. No marketing voice. No copywriter tricks. No superlatives. Just a woman telling her story.

FORMATTING:
Write in plain text only. Use ======== between major sections. Use ALL CAPS sparingly for emphasis — once or twice total. Use ellipses for trailing thoughts. Use contractions always (I'm, don't, can't). Short paragraphs — 1 to 3 sentences. Emoji bullets only in the CTA section. One continuous personal story flowing from hook to close.

PACING:
Every paragraph earns the reader's permission to read the next one. If a paragraph doesn't create tension, curiosity, or identification — cut it. Dead weight kills native ads. The best native ads feel like they're getting shorter as you read because every line pulls you forward.

WORD ALLOCATION:
60% on PAIN (Sections 1-5). 25% on DISCOVERY (Sections 6-8). 15% on FREEDOM (Sections 9-11). If you run long, cut from the back half. The pain sections are sacred.
